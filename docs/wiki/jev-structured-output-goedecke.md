# Jev means structured output is interesting again (Sean Goedecke)

## Summary

An independent engineering analysis of Jev by Sean Goedecke. He is enthusiastic about the
*interface* — fast, cheap structured output as a potential new computational primitive — but
skeptical of the *moat*. He argues Jev is "not that different from an ordinary LLM with structured
output," that fast structured output is already achievable by prefilling and single-token
constrained decoding, and that the "can't hallucinate" and "calibrated probabilities" claims are
partly semantic. A useful counterweight to the launch post's framing.

## Key Concepts

- **Autoregressive vs. single-pass** — LLMs generate structured output token-by-token (slow);
  Jev answers many questions in parallel in one forward pass (70 ms typical, 500 ms worst case).
- **Fast structured output as a new primitive** — the exciting part; injecting ~100 ms of cheap
  intelligence at decision points could enable non-chatbot programs (cf. Jev playing Doom).
- **The "no moat" thesis** — you can approximate Jev by prefilling `"choice": "` and generating a
  single constrained token against user-provided choices, batched via ordinary inference. This
  yields most of the speed/consistency/parallelism without a new model.
- **Skepticism on "can't hallucinate"** — a semantic dodge: Jev can still pick the *wrong* choice
  (call the sky "red"); constraining to a provided option space is something LLM structured
  output already does.
- **Skepticism on "calibrated"** — no evidence offered that Jev's probabilities are more than
  ordinary logit probabilities.
- **Intelligence ceiling** — with no test-time compute, Goedecke expects this class to cap around
  the strength of non-reasoning LLMs; not a new scaling axis.

## Details

Goedecke's biggest critique is methodological: the launch benchmarks force LLM baselines to emit
a full JSON blob token-by-token, which is the slow path. A fairer comparison would prefill and
constrain to a single decision token. He suspects the demos "could have been generated by
plugging any Terra-sized model into a single-token inference stack."

Despite the skepticism, he is net-positive: he wants fast-structured-output competition to exist
and hopes it pressures the big labs to ship fine-tuned variants (he floats a hypothetical
"GPT-5.6-Terra-System-One" as something he'd want to build products on). He also grants that
fine-tuning a model *specifically* for structured output is probably a real, if modest, edge.

### Contradictions

This source **directly disputes** two framings in the
[TypeSafe launch post](wiki/jev-launch-typesafe.md):

- **"Can't hallucinate"** — Goedecke: Jev can still choose wrongly; type-safety ≠ correctness, and
  LLM structured output already constrains to a schema.
- **"New scaling axis / RLCD moat"** — Goedecke: likely easy to replicate; not a brand-new axis
  and probably capped at non-reasoning-LLM intelligence.
